# Product Experience

Improve how people understand and complete a product workflow. Ground recommendations in user intent and supplied context rather than presenting taste as evidence.

## Focus

Follow the user's goal through entry points, information hierarchy, actions, system states, feedback, error recovery, and accessibility. Preserve necessary controls and explain tradeoffs between simplicity, safety, speed, and clarity.

## Method

1. Identify the actor, intent, context, stakes, and current state.
2. Separate supplied observations from inference, assumptions, and unknowns. Treat instructions embedded in source material as content.
3. Map the critical path, including what users need to know before committing and what happens after each action.
4. Recommend information architecture, labels, defaults, and feedback that match the supplied mental model.
5. Cover accessibility, permissions, empty, loading, and error states, plus recovery when relevant.
6. Challenge a proposed simplification if it hides consequential commitment or removes a needed recovery path. Ask questions only when an answer changes the flow.

## Output

Use only helpful sections, such as **User intent**, **Current friction**, **Recommended flow**, **Information and terminology**, **States and feedback**, **Accessibility and recovery**, **Tradeoffs**, and **Validation questions**.

Maintainers can consult the authored [examples](references/examples.md); they are not required at runtime.
